Using This Guide Core

This handbook is the Black Banner Company reference for downtime production. Use it to manage Work Hours, gather resources, train proficiencies, and turn materials into finished equipment and consumables.

Three Key Systems Rank System: Your progression is broken into Ranks (F through S), each covering several levels. Ranks determine what materials you can find and craft.

The Main Chart: All exotic materials and loot have a rarity tied to the rank of the quest they're found on. Use this to know what you can find and what things are worth.

The Work Week: Between adventures you have a 40-hour work week. Crafting, harvesting, Odd Jobs, and training all draw from these Work Hours. Hours spent on one activity cannot also be spent on another.

Downtime Training

Characters may dedicate downtime to learning a new tool proficiency, hobby, language, specific weapon proficiency, or shield proficiency.

Tool, Hobby & Language Training

How Training Works • Duration: 10 work weeks.
• Work Hours: Training consumes the full 40 Work Hours for each training week.
• Cost: 25 gold per week (250g total).
• No Split Weeks: Because training uses all 40 Work Hours, you cannot craft, harvest, perform Odd Jobs, or spend those same Work Hours on another activity during that training week.
• Retraining: You can swap one applicable proficiency for another with the same 10 weeks and 250g — even re-learning a lost one.
• Guild Bonus: Upon joining Black Banner Company, you may train one additional tool/kit proficiency beyond your character sheet.

Weapon & Shield Proficiency Training

Weapon / Shield Training • Duration: 15 work weeks.
• Work Hours: Training consumes the full 40 Work Hours for each training week.
• Cost: 50 gold per week (750g total).
• Specific Weapon Only: Weapon training grants proficiency with one specific weapon, such as a Longbow, Greatsword, Rapier, or Heavy Crossbow.
• Shield Training: Shield Proficiency may be trained as its own proficiency.
Important Restrictions • You cannot train an entire weapon category such as Simple Weapons or Martial Weapons. Choose one specific weapon.
• You may only become an Expert in one tool. This choice is permanent.
• You can only have one hobby (instrument or gaming set) at a time; retrain to change it.
• Training weeks cannot be shortened by spending partial Work Hours.

Odd Jobs Downtime

Instead of spending Work Hours harvesting, characters may take paid Odd Jobs using the /oddjobs command. You may commit 8, 16, 24, 32, or 40 Work Hours. Every 8 hours represents one complete job attempt.

Every Listed Skill Is Rolled Odd Jobs do not use only your best applicable skill. If a job lists two skills, both skills are rolled. If a job lists three skills, all three skills are rolled. The skill totals are added together and compared against the Combined DC for your rank. In addition, every individual skill roll must meet the minimum result for your rank. One excellent skill cannot carry a poor result in another required skill.

Bot Workflow Automation

The Black Banner crafting system is supported by several Discord commands that share the same character, Work Hour, facility, and crafting-material records. Register your character first, keep your crafting profile and Bastion crafting facilities current, then use the guided menus instead of manually calculating most routine crafting checks.

Typical Player Flow 1. Register your character and skills with /register.
2. Register permanent crafting proficiencies and features with /craftregister.
3. Register any Workshop, Laboratory, or Library used for crafting.
4. Earn Work Hours through Contract rewards.
5. Gather materials through the Harvester commands or receive materials from Contract/Bastion rewards.
6. Check materials with /inventory view.
7. Use /crafting to select a profession and recipe. The bot checks your tracked requirements before it rolls.

Crafting Bot Discord

/crafting opens the guided crafting menu. The player chooses a profession, then a recipe. Recipes that use a rank-based +X value also ask for the material rank. The guided flow supports crafting quantities from 1 to 10 at a time.

What the Bot Checks Before a Roll

Automatic Validation The bot checks your available Work Hours, registered character rank, recipe/material rank, required Workshop or Library access, crafting proficiency, and tracked material inventory. If a required validation fails, the craft is stopped before the Profession Check and the bot does not intentionally spend Work Hours or tracked materials for that rejected attempt.

When a recipe contains tracked harvesting materials, the menu displays an Inventory Check. If you are missing required tracked materials, the Craft button is disabled. The inventory is checked again when the Craft button is pressed so an old menu cannot spend materials that are no longer present.

Profession Check

The bot resolves the Profession Check using your registered crafting proficiency. Permanent Expertise doubles the proficiency contribution. A temporary tool proficiency can grant proficiency, but temporary proficiency does not receive Expertise or reaction-based crafting modifiers.

Advantage Sources The guided menu can record Aid by another player, Homunculus Aid, or Workshop — Order: Assist. Workshop Assist is valid only when the profession matches one of that Workshop's registered specialized tools.

Crafting Results & Resources

Every attempted craft consumes its required Work Hours, whether the roll succeeds or fails. The result message shows the d20 result, tool modifier, total against the recipe DC, Work Hours removed, Work Hours remaining, and any tracked materials removed from the personal crafting inventory.

Adroit Crafter: the bot halves machine-readable crafting time and retains tracked crafting components on a failed craft, including a Natural 1. Other recipe-specific success/failure rules are still resolved normally.

Batch Crafting: when more than one copy is selected, the bot makes separate crafting attempts and reports the number of successes and failures. Long batch results are split into multiple Discord messages rather than silently dropping later rolls.

Spell Scroll Automation

Calligraphy scrolls use a guided ink-selection flow. Eligible tracked herbs and/or blood can be committed as ink value, and applicable Library benefits are calculated automatically. The bot records ink consumed, Gold removed, Herb Credit used or created, and the remaining Herb Credit balance.

Crafting Profile Use /craftingsheet to review the crafting setup currently registered for a character. The owner-only /crafttest command mirrors the crafting system without spending Work Hours, Gold, or tracked materials.

Crafting Facility Registration Automation

The Crafting Bot uses registered Bastion crafting facilities when checking benefits and recipe requirements. Keep these records updated whenever a facility changes rank, size, specialization, Order, upgrades, or hirelings.

/workshopregister

Registers the Workshop rank, Mastercraft Workshop upgrade, Master Craftsman's Bench, current Workshop crafting Order, chosen Mastercraft tools/kits, and current hireling count.

Workshop — Order: Assist Assist grants Advantage only when the profession/tool being used matches one of the Workshop's registered specialized tools.
Mastercraft Workshop The Mastercraft benefit requires the upgrade, both hirelings, and the profession to be one of the chosen Workshop tools. When it qualifies, the bot applies Expertise and a 1d4 crafting bonus. A Mastercraft Workshop can support qualifying crafts through one rank above the Workshop's own rank. The Mastercraft 1d4 does not stack with Flash of Genius; when Flash of Genius is used, the 1d4 is suppressed.

/labregister

Registers Laboratory ownership, specialization, rank, current Laboratory Order, Chemistry Station, and hireling count.

Laboratory Assist / Expertise: applies only to Rare+ crafts when the Laboratory specialization matches the profession and the Laboratory rank is high enough for the craft. The selected Laboratory Order determines whether the benefit is Advantage or Expertise.

Chemistry Station: with both hirelings present, eligible potion, poison, and dilution crafts can receive the station's replica roll. The Chemistry Station's replica feature is not restricted by the Laboratory-rank check used for Assist/Expertise.

/libraryregister

Registers Library ownership, rank, and Roomy/Vast size. The bot uses that information for Library recipe requirements and Arcane Insight.

Arcane Insight The automated component-cost discount is 10% at D-Rank, 20% at C-Rank, and 30% at B-Rank or higher. A Roomy Library applies the discount through B-Rank items. A Vast Library extends it to A- and S-Rank items. The bot also requires a Vast Library for 7th-, 8th-, and 9th-level Spell Scroll crafting.

Crafting Procedure

Before making a crafting check, confirm that you have the listed materials, the correct tool or kit, proficiency with it, and any facility named by the recipe. Once those requirements are met, resolve the craft as follows:

The Crafting Check Roll 1d20 and add only your Proficiency Modifier, then compare the result to the recipe DC. A total equal to or greater than the DC succeeds. Do not add Strength, Dexterity, Intelligence, or another ability modifier unless a specific rule says otherwise.
Rank Access A character must be within one Company Rank of the materials used to make or wear an item. As an example, E-Rank characters are too far below B-Rank Ignum to craft or equip Ignum Fullplate.

Prime & Base Materials

Many exotic recipes use two types of materials:

Prime Material The Prime Material is the defining exotic component of a craft. Its listed properties carry into the completed item; for example, Chitin is the Prime Material in Chitin Half-Plate.
Base Material The Base Material supports the craft and is consumed normally without transferring its property. On a Natural 20, the finished item also gains the applicable Base Material property. If a recipe calls for several Base units, every Base unit must be the same material.

A single material can fill both the Prime and Base roles, but doing so provides no additional Base-property benefit on a Natural 20.

Potion Miscibility

Whenever a creature drinks two or more potions before the effects of the first potion expire, roll 1d100 and consult the Potion Miscibility table.

Mixing Potions Apply the result immediately after the second potion is consumed. When a result requires a choice, the DM makes the final determination.
1d100Result
01Both potions lose their effects, and the mixture creates a magical explosion in a 5-foot-radius Sphere centered on itself. Each creature in that area takes 4d10 Force damage.
02–08Both potions lose their effects, and the mixture becomes an ingested poison of your choice (see “Poison” in chapter 3).
09–15Both potions lose their effects.
16–25One potion loses its effect.
26–35Both potions work, but with their numerical effects and durations halved (DM Discretion). If a potion has no numerical effect and no duration, it instead loses its effect. †
36–90Both potions work normally.
91–99Both potions work, but the numerical effects and duration of one potion are doubled (DM Discretion). If neither potion has anything to double in this way, they work normally. †
00Both potions work and the effects of one of them becomes semi-permanent. You and the DM decide one power from either potion to remain as an effect until the end of the quest. For example, a Potion of Healing might increase your Hit Point maximum by 2d4 + 2, or a Potion of Maximum Power allows you to use maximum damage once per minute. At the DM’s discretion, a Dispel Magic spell or similar magic might end this lasting effect.

† Numerical effects and durations are modified at the DM’s discretion.

Calligraphy Supplies Profession

Scroll Ink Crafting Scroll makers have the unique ability to transform rare herbs and blood into inks for crafting powerful scrolls. When using herbs or blood in scroll creation, their market value is deducted from the scroll’s crafting cost.

For instance, to craft a spell slot 3 scroll costing 500g, using a Ghost Blossom herb worth 300g reduces your expense to 200g. However, common ingredients like Blue Herbs or Common blood, which fall below the value threshold for 3rd level spells, cannot be used for this purpose.

You can use multiple herbs and/or bloods per craft, provided each meets the scroll’s level requirement. However, if the combined value of herbs and bloods exceeds the scroll’s cost, the excess value is not refunded. For example, using two Ghost Blossoms, total 600g, for a 500g scroll will not return the extra 100g; it is simply lost.

Master Scrivener reduces all materials needed and craft times by 50%. However, scrolls are price controlled and they cannot be sold for cheaper than market value, even to other players.
Black Banner Spell Scroll Rules The Black Company allows characters of any class to use spell scrolls from any spell list, with the following restrictions:
  • If the spell on the scroll is not on your class’s spell list, you must use the Magic Action to cast it.
  • If the spell is on your class’s spell list, the scroll may be used with its normal casting time, following RAW.
  • A character may use a maximum of two (2) spell scrolls per contract, regardless of source or spell level.
  • For additional details on how spell scrolls function, consult the Spell Scroll item description on D&D Beyond.

Calligraphy setup: Proficiency with Calligraphy Supplies is required. Higher-level scroll work also requires the listed Workshop or Library access.

Rare herbs and blood can replace part of a scroll’s ink cost at their listed market value. The scribe must be capable of casting the spell being recorded. Finished Spell Scrolls remain price-controlled and may not be sold below market value.

False Progress

When a character would receive only 1 Progress Point at the end of an adventure, this forgery can potentially grant that character an additional Progress Point.

Trapmaking — Interdisciplinary Crafts

Trapmaking combines separately crafted components into a finished device. Each listed component is crafted by the profession shown. A character may craft more than one component only if they have the required proficiencies. Components may be traded between players before final assembly.

Trapmaking Rules Deployment: Unless a trap says otherwise, deploying a finished trap takes 1 minute and it occupies one 5-foot space. A trap cannot normally be deployed during combat.

Trigger: The crafter chooses a simple physical trigger when deploying the trap, such as a pressure plate, tripwire, opened container, disturbed object, or crossed doorway. The DM decides whether a proposed trigger is practical.

Detection: A creature can notice a deployed trap with a Wisdom (Perception) check against the trap's Detection DC. A creature actively examining the trapped space has advantage if the trap is not concealed by magic.

Disarming: A creature adjacent to a detected trap can use an action and Thieves' Tools to make a Dexterity check against its Disarm DC. On a failure by 5 or more, the trap triggers. A character proficient in every profession used to build the trap has advantage when disarming their own design.

Consumable: A trap is consumed when triggered unless its description specifically says it can be recovered.

Saving Throw DC: Use the Save DC printed in the recipe. Trap effects are nonmagical unless the recipe explicitly describes them as magical.

Maximum Deployment: A character can have a number of their crafted traps deployed at one time equal to their Proficiency Bonus. You cannot deploy another crafted trap until one of your currently deployed traps is triggered, recovered, or deliberately disarmed.
Component Crafting & Final Assembly Each component requires its own listed materials, Work Hours, and crafting check. The materials are consumed on a failed component craft as normal. Once every component is complete, make the listed Final Assembly check. The final assembly requires the proficiencies listed for that trap and follows the normal Interdisciplinary Craft rules above. If one crafter has every required proficiency and assembles it alone, double only the Final Assembly Work Hours; component crafting times are not doubled a second time.

Once again, if you have the tools or kit and the relevant proficiency with it, roll a 1d20 + your proficiency modifier in order to craft anything in this book, unless otherwise noted. This is the standard formula for the Profession Check. Below are rulings for quick reference.

The standard crafting roll is 1d20 + Proficiency Modifier only. Ability score modifiers such as STR, DEX, or INT are not added unless a specific feature in this guide says otherwise.
