What is a Bastion? Overview

A Bastion is a location that belongs to your character: a base, stronghold, ship, hideout, temple, tower, keep, casino, lodge, or other seat of power. It offers refuge from adventuring and gives access to crafting bonuses, harvesting, defenders, hirelings, buffs, and story events.

A Bastion is also a creative playground. It gives your character a storytelling space where pets, NPC friends, servants, hirelings, allies, and personal projects can have a home and become part of the world.

When Can I Start?Build your first Basic Facility at D-Rank / Level 6. Special Facilities begin at C-Rank / Level 9.

Building & Costs Construction

Starting Your BastionTo start your Bastion, build any Basic Facility. Once that first facility is completed, your Bastion officially exists. All Basic Facilities begin at D-Rank and Cramped size.

Basic Facilities are rooms every Bastion can have, while Special Facilities are more specialized rooms that expand your character’s skills, story, or influence. You may build one of every Basic Facility unless a rule says otherwise. Some facilities, such as additional Barracks beyond the first, use Special Facility slots even though they continue to upgrade like Basic Facilities.

You may only build one facility at a time. Finish the current facility before beginning another so construction progress remains clear.

Combined Bastion ContributionsResidents may help pay for another player's construction or upgrades, but payment never changes facility ownership. See Sharing a Bastion for the full ownership and sharing rules.

Cost Reductions

  • Charisma: 13+ = –25%, 15+ = –50%, 17+ = –75%, 19+ = free. Magic items count.
  • Disguise Kit: Proficient = –10%; Expertise = –25%.
  • Costs cannot be reduced below 0.

Moving Hirelings

You can move a hireling from any basic or special facility to another within your Bastion as long as the facilities are the same rank. If moving a hireling into a higher-ranked room, pay the cost difference. If your total reduction is 100% or more, moving them is free.

  1. At the start of your Bastion Turn, hire new hirelings, move existing hirelings, or upgrade them.
  2. Roll your Individual Bastion Turn event.
  3. After rolling, you cannot hire new hirelings until your next Bastion Turn.
  4. You may still move or upgrade existing hirelings until the beginning of your next Bastion Turn.

Hirelings cannot be lent. If a non-Acolyte hireling is given to another player for roleplay purposes, that player treats the hireling as a new acquisition and pays the normal cost each time the hireling changes hands.

Acolytes as Hirelings

If you have an Acolyte, they can work as a hireling as long as they meet the facility prerequisites. Acolytes work for free and can work in a room up to one rank above their own. If an event would kill or lose your Acolyte, they cannot work in any facility until the beginning of your next Individual Bastion Turn.

Bastion Turns Core Rules

Bastion Turn SummaryMonthly Bastion Turns happen on the first day of each real-life month and affect all players. Individual Bastion Turns are gained after completing every Contract. Construction Turns happen every workweek reset and are used to build or upgrade facilities.

There are two kinds of Bastion Turns: Monthly Bastion Turns and Individual Bastion Turns.

  • Monthly Bastion Turn: Happens on the first day of every real-life month and is rolled by a Dungeon Master.
  • Individual Bastion Turn: You gain one Individual Bastion Turn after completing every Contract. Bastion Turns do not stack; if you complete another Contract before using your pending turn, the unused turn is lost and replaced by the new one.
Bastion OrdersOnce per Individual Bastion Turn, you can issue one order to each one of your facilities if applicable.

Sharing a Bastion Combined Bastions

Up to six players may live together in a Combined Bastion. Sharing a Bastion means sharing a stronghold, not sharing character progression. Each facility still has one owner, and that owner retains control of the facility regardless of who helped pay for it.

The Golden RuleYou may share the home, share construction costs, and receive benefits that are specifically allowed to be shared. You do not automatically share ownership, Expertise, crafting specialization, Orders, hirelings, Acolytes, or other character-specific progression.

Residents & Shared Buffs

Characters sharing a Combined Bastion must be officially listed as residents. Being in the same contract, adventuring party, or visiting before a contract does not make a character a resident.

No Pre-Contract Buff StationAll Bastion buffs that can be shared may only be shared with characters officially listed as living in that Combined Bastion, unless the facility specifically states otherwise. Players may not temporarily add someone as a resident simply to give them a Bastion buff before a contract.

Facility Ownership & Paying Together

Residents may contribute gold toward another resident's construction, rank upgrades, size upgrades, special upgrades, repairs, or other improvements. Paying toward a facility never grants ownership. The facility belongs entirely to the character whose Bastion it was built for, regardless of who paid or how the cost was divided.

ExampleThorne owns a Workshop that costs 4,000 GP to upgrade. Thorne pays 1,000 GP, Lyra pays 1,000 GP, and Kael pays 2,000 GP. The Workshop still belongs 100% to Thorne.

Leaving a Combined Bastion

If you leave a Combined Bastion to establish or join another Bastion, you retain only facilities that belong to your own Bastion. Facilities owned by another player do not transfer with you, even if you helped pay for them. Contributions are not automatically refunded and facilities cannot be divided according to who paid what. Ownership follows the Bastion, not the gold.

What Sharing a Room Allows

  • Enter and use the physical space with the owner's permission.
  • Roleplay, sleep, eat, store appropriate belongings, or use ordinary furnishings when the room and its capacity allow it.
  • Receive a mechanical benefit only when that benefit specifically states that it affects residents, other players, allies, guests, the Combined Bastion, or creatures within it.
  • Receive appropriate Bastion-wide defensive effects when the facility's rules allow it.

What Sharing a Room Does Not Allow

Physical access does not grant the owner's character-specific benefits. Unless a facility specifically says otherwise, you do not gain another player's:

  • Tool or kit Expertise or proficiency.
  • Workshop specialization, Assist benefit, Mastercraft benefits, or personal crafting bonuses.
  • Personal crafting benefits from a Laboratory, Kitchen, or similar facility.
  • Facility Orders or additional Orders.
  • Hirelings or Acolytes.
  • Special Facility slots or ownership.
  • Character-specific buffs, progression, prerequisites, or upgrades.

Workshops & Duplicate Special Facilities

A Workshop's Expertise and specialization belong to the Workshop owner. Another resident may physically use the Workshop for appropriate crafting, but uses their own proficiencies, Expertise, and bonuses. They do not gain the owner's Workshop Expertise, Assist benefit, Mastercraft bonuses, or specialization.

A character may not construct multiple copies of the same Special Facility merely to collect additional character benefits unless that facility specifically states that multiple copies are allowed. The Workshop's Respecialize and Mastercraft Workshop features are its intended progression for additional specializations.

Orders, Hirelings & Acolytes

Each character issues Orders only to facilities they own. Another resident cannot use your unused Order or issue an Order to your facility for you. Hirelings remain attached to their owner's Bastion and cannot be lent to staff or activate another player's facilities. Acolytes likewise remain attached to their character and are not communal hirelings.

Rank Restrictions Still Apply

Sharing never bypasses rank requirements. A shareable benefit that scales by rank grants the receiving resident no more than the benefit appropriate to their own current rank unless the facility specifically states otherwise. A facility that must match a character's rank to function must still meet that requirement.

Defense

Facilities that protect the physical stronghold may protect the Combined Bastion when their rules allow it. Defenders remain owned by their original Bastion but may be temporarily deployed to block an attack or lift a siege. Facilities such as siege weapons that explicitly allow use for another member of a Combined Bastion work as written.

If an Owner's Bastion Is Lost

Helping pay for another player's facilities does not give inheritance rights. If the owner dies or retires and an eligible Acolyte inherits, the Bastion may continue through Transference. If no eligible successor inherits and the Bastion is abandoned, its facilities are lost even if other residents contributed gold. If that Bastion was the shared home, the remaining residents must establish, relocate to, or join another Bastion.

Facility Sharing Labels

Every Basic and Special Facility now has a Sharing: line. Use that line as the first authority for who can receive the facility’s mechanical benefits. The detailed facility text then explains any limits or exceptions.

  • Owner Only: Mechanical benefits belong to the owner.
  • Combined Bastion Residents: Eligible benefits may be given only to officially listed residents.
  • Bastion-Wide: The stated effect protects or affects the shared stronghold as described.
  • Physical Facility Shared: Residents may use the space normally, but do not inherit the owner’s character-specific bonuses.
  • Limited Combined Bastion Support: Only the specific shared interaction written in that facility is allowed.

Bastion Legacy & Transference Long-Term Investment

A Bastion can be a permanent long-term investment for your future characters on the server when you maintain the proper Acolyte succession path. Gold, Construction Turns, facilities, and permanent improvements can potentially remain useful through generations of your characters.

The Succession PathMain Character → Acolyte → New Main → New Acolyte → Future Main

How Transference Works

  1. Your Main builds and develops the Bastion normally.
  2. If the Main dies or retires and you choose their eligible Acolyte as your next Main, the Acolyte may inherit the Bastion.
  3. The heir may benefit from previously built facilities only at their current rank. Higher-rank facilities remain physically present but locked until the heir reaches the required rank.
  4. When the new Main is or reaches B-Rank, they may create a new Acolyte under the normal Acolyte rules.
  5. If that Main later dies or retires, the new Acolyte may become the next Main and inherit the Bastion, continuing the succession.

Inherited Facilities

Inherited facilities do not need to be rebuilt merely because ownership changed. Inheritance preserves the Bastion, not the previous character's rank or personal progression.

  • The heir can benefit from inherited facilities at or below their current rank, subject to normal prerequisites and rules.
  • Facilities above the heir's rank remain locked until the heir reaches the required rank.
  • Any rank-scaled benefit is limited to the heir's current rank.
  • To replace an inherited facility, destroy the old facility and pay the normal gold cost and construction time for the replacement.

Breaking the Succession

If your Main dies or retires and you do not continue with an eligible Acolyte as your next Main, the Bastion is considered abandoned. It cannot be transferred directly to an unrelated new character. Combined Bastion residents do not inherit your facilities because they lived there, used them, or helped pay for them. Your Bastion follows your succession line.

Your Character's LegacyBuild for the character you play today, but remember that you may also be building for the characters you play tomorrow. A properly maintained Bastion can pass from Main to Acolyte for generations.

Defending Your Bastion Combat

Some events send attackers against your Bastion, threatening to destroy facilities and kill everyone inside. Battlements, Defenders, Armories, and siege weapons are the primary tools used to withstand these attacks.

You cannot be attacked until you have at least four facilities of any type. If an attack event occurs before then, the enemy ignores your Bastion.

Barracks, Graveyards, and Menageries allow you to hire or house Defenders. Some events can grant extra Defenders who provide their own housing, such as Mercenary Guest, Ronin, or Duel to the Death.

Hiring Defenders from a Barracks does not cost gold, though Defenders gained from other events or facilities might.

Defender Combat

Bastion combat works by rolling a Defender’s combat die. If the result is within that Defender’s death threshold, the Defender dies but still kills the attacker they blocked.

  • Normal Barracks and Graveyard Defenders use a d6 and normally die on a 3 or lower.
  • Beasts from a Menagerie may use anything from a d6 to a d12.
  • Knights use a d12.
  • Battlements, an Armory, and other Bastion features can reduce the death threshold.
Event Combat NoteUnless otherwise stated, any event that requires a Defender to roll a combat die uses the normal Bastion combat rules. A Defender that rolls within their death threshold is killed. Improvements from Battlements, Armory, or other Bastion features apply when relevant.

A Defender blocks one attacker at a time. If the Defender survives, they may immediately block another attacker. For ease of play, roll a number of combat dice equal to the number of attackers and count through the results until either all attackers are blocked or all available Defenders are dead.

Combat ExampleA force of 6 attackers assaults a Bastion with 4 d6 Defenders and fully upgraded Battlements. Roll 6d6, one die per attacker. Suppose the results are 5, 5, 4, 5, 1, and 6. Fully upgraded Battlements reduce the Defenders’ death threshold to 1. The first four attacks are blocked, one Defender dies on the fifth result, and the surviving Defenders block the sixth attacker. The Bastion is victorious.

If fully upgraded Battlements and the appropriate Armory upgrades cause your home Defenders to fight with d8s, roll d8s instead of d6s when defending your own Bastion. Different Defender types, such as Knights using d12s, are best rolled before or after the normal Defenders so their results remain easy to track.

It is statistically possible for a single surviving Bastion Defender to hold off a very large force because they may continue blocking attackers until they roll within their death threshold.

Repelling Attacks

You may sacrifice Battlements to repel an attack after your Defenders are killed, or release a Friendly Monster to drive the attackers away. Repelling an attack by destroying Battlements or using a similar automatic defense grants no Reward.

Siege Weapons

Siege weapons block a set number of attackers during normal attacks or deal damage during an Invasion.

  • Ballista: Kills 6 attackers or deals 3d10 damage during an Invasion.
  • Catapult: Kills 8 attackers or deals 10d6 damage during an Invasion.
  • Cannon: Kills 16 attackers or deals 8d10 damage during an Invasion.

Drought

Invasion

A hostile kingdom marches toward Drathmere. On the 15th day of the month, all players may send Defenders to withstand the assault. One player’s Bastion is chosen as the war site, and facilities that normally affect only that player’s Defenders now affect everyone until the war ends.

Once the event begins counting on the 15th, no more forces can be sent. Each player rolls the combat dice for all sent Defenders and totals the results. A Friendly Monster fights with 10d12 instead of repelling the event. Siege weapons may be used.

  • 10,000+: The Invasion is crushed. All participating players roll twice on the Reward table.
  • 5,000+: The Invasion is repelled. Half of each participating player’s Defenders are killed, chosen by that player. Roll once on the Reward table.
  • Below 5,000: Drathmere is defeated. All sent Bastion Defenders are killed and each participating player’s Battlements are destroyed.
Search Party

A VIP has disappeared and the guild forms a search party. The search can happen any time during the month. Players send Defenders, roll each Defender’s combat die, total all results, and submit the total to a DM.

You cannot send siege weapons, but you can use War Room. If the combined total is 1,000 or more, the VIP is found. Everyone who sent at least one Defender rolls on the Reward table for their rank. If one player contributed 50% or more of the total, that player rolls twice.

Duel to the Death

A wandering knight arrives, boasting he is the greatest swordsman in Thracia. He challenges your strongest Defender to a duel.

Choose one Defender as your champion. Siege weapons and Friendly Monsters cannot participate. Roll your champion’s combat die three times. Normal Defenders use 1d6, Knights use 1d12, and other Defenders use their normal combat die.

Your champion follows normal Bastion combat rules and dies if they roll within their death range. For example, a normal Defender dies on a roll of 3 or lower. A Defender protected by Battlements or Armory improvements may have a lower death threshold.

If your champion dies during any of the three rolls, you lose and the knight departs. If your champion survives all three rolls, you win and the knight joins your Bastion as a Knight Defender using 1d12. The knight takes care of their own accommodations.

Guildhall Level 20 Capstone

Founding a Guild — 100,000 GPOnly a Level 20 player character may found a Guild. Establishing the organization and constructing its Guildhall costs 100,000 GP. This is a founding cost and is not deposited into the Guild Treasury. A newly founded Guild begins with a Treasury balance of 0 GP.

The founding cost represents constructing or securing the Guildhall, recruiting the initial staff, acquiring equipment and supplies, and establishing the organization as a functioning power within the Black Banner Company.

No Special Facility Slot: A Guildhall is a separate Level 20 capstone facility and does not consume a C-, B-, A-, or S-Rank Special Facility slot.

One Guild Type Per Server: Only one of each established Guild type may exist on the server at a time. Once a Warriors' Guild, Mages' Guild, Thieves' Guild, Assassins' Guild, Adventurers' Guild, Artisans' Guild, or Entertainers' Guild has been founded, another player cannot found a second Guild of that type.

Guild Master: The Level 20 character who pays the founding cost and creates the Guild becomes its Guild Master. The Guild Master may recruit other player characters into the Guild.

Membership: A player character may belong to only one Guild at a time. Guild names describe the organization and training it provides, not a class requirement. A Wizard could join the Warriors' Guild, a Fighter could join the Mages' Guild, or any other character could join a Guild that makes sense for that character.

Building the Guild

A newly created Guild begins at C-Rank. The Guild Master builds the organization by recruiting active player characters. As the Guild grows, it can advance from C-Rank to B-Rank, A-Rank, and finally S-Rank.

Guild RankRecruitment Requirement
CGuild founded by a Level 20 character after paying the 100,000 GP founding cost.
BFounder + 3 active members, including at least 1 B-Rank or higher member.
AFounder + 5 active members, including at least 2 A-Rank or higher members.
SFounder + 7 active members, including at least 3 S-Rank members.

Member Benefit Rank: Guild benefits begin at C-Rank. A member receives the benefit corresponding to their own Black Banner rank, capped by the Guild's current rank. A C-Rank member in an S-Rank Guild receives the C-Rank benefit; a B-Rank member receives the B-Rank benefit; and so on. A member can never receive a Guild benefit above their own rank or above the Guild's current rank.

Guild Treasury

Each Guild maintains its own Guild Bank/Treasury, separate from individual player Gold and other Guilds. The Treasury begins at 0 GP when the Guild is founded.

  • Guild members may donate Gold to their Guild Treasury.
  • Gold deposited into the Treasury belongs to the Guild and is not an individual member's personal balance.
  • Only the Guild Master may authorize withdrawals or spending from the Treasury unless a DM-approved Guild rule specifically states otherwise.
  • Guild deposits, withdrawals, and spending should be recorded so the Guild's balance can be tracked.

Custom Guilds

A Level 20 character may propose a Custom Guild instead of selecting one of the established Guild types. The founder must submit the Guild's name, theme, purpose, Assignment, recruitment concept, and C/B/A/S member benefits to a DM for approval before the Guild can be founded.

Custom Guild benefits must be comparable in power to the established Guild options. An approved Custom Guild follows the same Level 20 requirement, 100,000 GP founding cost, recruitment progression, Treasury rules, and membership rules. Each approved Custom Guild is treated as its own unique Guild type, and that exact Custom Guild cannot be duplicated elsewhere on the server.

Guild Types & Member Benefits Guildhall

Each established Guild has an Assignment that reflects the work performed by the organization and a C/B/A/S training progression for its player members.

Warriors' Guild

Focus: Martial characters, soldiers, guards, weapon masters, and anyone seeking formal combat training.

Assignment — Muster the Company: Send Guild warriors to protect a settlement, caravan, Bastion, or other location. After one Bastion Turn, the Guild receives 500 GP for its Treasury, or the DM may substitute an appropriate favor or story reward.

C — Combat Drills: Once per turn, you gain +1 to one weapon attack roll you make.

B — Veteran Training: Once per turn, you gain +1 to one weapon attack roll you make and +1 to that attack's damage if it hits.

A — Battle-Hardened: Once per turn, you gain +1 to one weapon attack roll you make and +2 to that attack's damage if it hits.

S — Master-at-Arms: Once per turn, you gain +1 to one weapon attack roll you make and +3 to that attack's damage if it hits. Once per Contract, you may reroll one missed weapon attack.

Mages' Guild

Focus: Spellcasters, magical researchers, occult scholars, and characters devoted to mastering magic.

Assignment — Arcane Research: Commission Guild scholars to research a spell, magical phenomenon, creature, plane, or magical object. After one Bastion Turn, the Guild Master receives up to three useful pieces of information the DM determines the researchers could reasonably uncover.

C — Arcane Training: You gain +1 to spell attack rolls.

B — Spell Recovery: You gain +1 to spell attack rolls. Once per Contract, regain one expended 1st-level spell slot.

A — Greater Recovery: You gain +1 to spell attack rolls. Once per Contract, regain one expended spell slot of 2nd level or lower.

S — Master Arcanist: You gain +1 to spell attack rolls. Once per Contract, regain one expended spell slot of 3rd level or lower.

Thieves' Guild

Focus: Rogues, burglars, smugglers, scouts, spies, and specialists in stealth and acquisition.

Assignment — Acquire: Guild agents steal or recover a nonmagical object worth up to 500 GP within the local region, provided the DM determines the target is reasonably accessible.

C — Light Fingers: +1 to Sleight of Hand checks.

B — Shadow Training: +1 to Sleight of Hand and Stealth checks.

A — Master Thief: +2 to Sleight of Hand and Stealth checks.

S — Ghost: +2 to Sleight of Hand and Stealth checks. Once per Contract, reroll one failed Sleight of Hand or Stealth check.

Assassins' Guild

Focus: Assassins, infiltrators, bounty hunters, spies, and specialists in eliminating dangerous targets.

Assignment — Gather Intelligence: Guild agents investigate a named target or organization. After one Bastion Turn, the Guild Master receives up to three useful pieces of information the DM determines the agents could reasonably uncover.

C — Killer's Instinct: +1 to Initiative.

B — Exploit Opening: +2 to Initiative. Once per Contract, when you hit a creature that has not yet taken a turn in that combat, add your Proficiency Bonus to the damage.

A — Death Mark: +3 to Initiative. Once per Contract, when you hit a creature that has not yet taken a turn in that combat, deal an additional 2d6 damage.

S — Master Assassin: +4 to Initiative. Once per Contract, when you hit a creature that has not yet taken a turn in that combat, deal an additional 4d6 damage.

Adventurers' Guild

Focus: Explorers, monster hunters, scouts, treasure seekers, and general adventurers.

Assignment — Monster Hunt: Guild adventurers locate a Beast or Monstrosity of CR 3 or lower within the region and kill or capture it after one Bastion Turn. An eligible captured creature may be transferred to a Menagerie if the Menagerie rules allow it.

C — Pathfinder: +1 to Survival checks.

B — Seasoned Explorer: +1 to Survival and Perception checks.

A — Veteran Adventurer: +2 to Survival and Perception checks.

S — Legendary Explorer: +2 to Survival and Perception checks. Once per Contract, reroll one failed Survival or Perception check.

Artisans' Guild

Focus: Crafters, tradespeople, smiths, alchemists, herbalists, jewellers, masons, and other profession-focused characters.

Assignment — Commission: Choose a crafting profession represented among the Guild's members. The Guild completes mundane commissions during the Bastion Turn, generating 500 GP for the Guild Treasury.

C — Apprentice Network: Choose one profession in which you are proficient when joining the Guild. You gain +1 to checks with that profession.

B — Journeyman Network: The bonus increases to +2.

A — Master Network: The bonus increases to +3.

S — Grandmaster Network: The bonus increases to +4. Once per Contract or workweek, reroll one failed check with that profession.

Guild membership does not grant profession proficiency; you must already possess the required proficiency to receive this benefit.

Entertainers' Guild

Focus: Bards, musicians, actors, storytellers, diplomats, public speakers, and socially focused characters.

Assignment — Grand Performance: Guild performers organize a concert, play, festival, or other major event. After one Bastion Turn, the Guild receives 500 GP for its Treasury, or the DM may substitute an appropriate social favor.

C — Performer: +1 to Performance checks.

B — Crowd Favorite: +1 to Performance and Persuasion checks.

A — Celebrity: +2 to Performance and Persuasion checks.

S — Living Legend: +2 to Performance and Persuasion checks. Once per Contract, reroll one failed Performance or Persuasion check.

Custom Guild

DM Approval Required. A Custom Guild may represent an organization not covered by the seven established Guild types. Its Assignment and C/B/A/S member benefits are designed with the founding player and must receive DM approval before the Guild is founded or used.

A Custom Guild follows all normal Guild rules, including the Level 20 requirement, 100,000 GP founding cost, Guild Treasury, recruitment progression, one-Guild membership limit, and unique Guild type restriction.
